# Preston Wimberly Creative Direction

## Mission

Create work that feels trusted before it feels admired.

Use this operating formula:

**material truth x editorial judgment x function x regional character x precise finish**

Keep the finish. Lose the costume. Treat this as a decision system, not a vintage aesthetic or inspiration collage.

## Establish the brief

Before designing, writing, editing, or coding, determine:

1. State what the work is actually about in one sentence without branding language.
2. Identify the evidence: real people, objects, photographs, documents, numbers, materials, places, dates, constraints, and outcomes.
3. Choose only three or four target characteristics from timeless, authentic, masculine, expensive, honest, reliable, and capable.
4. Select only the reference systems that solve the current problem.
5. Decide what should lead: image, story, object, data, person, or place.
6. Identify what can be removed without reducing understanding.
7. Test whether the work remains distinctive without its logo.

If the source material, audience, factual claims, rights, or conversion goal are unclear, inspect the available evidence before proposing a visual direction. Do not invent missing provenance or outcomes.

## Make every choice earn its place

Retain a choice only when it is justified by at least one of these:

- **Use:** Helps someone act, understand, navigate, compare, repair, or remember.
- **Place:** Expresses geography, institution, weather, light, architecture, or local culture that materially changes the work.
- **Craft:** Reveals how something was built, joined, tuned, maintained, finished, or worn.
- **Story:** Reveals a real person, decision, chronology, constraint, consequence, or outcome.

Remove elements that cannot be defended by use, place, craft, or story.

## Apply the creative standard

- Make **timeless** mean durable after ten years of real use.
- Make **authentic** mean rooted in actual people, place, work, and materials.
- Make **masculine** mean direct, restrained, and competent; never macho or theatrical.
- Make **expensive** emerge from proportion, editing, photography, material, pacing, and finish; never ornament alone.
- Make **honest** mean claims are supported by proof and specific nouns replace inflated adjectives.
- Make **reliable** mean orderly, legible, consistent, and calm under pressure.
- Make **capable** mean the work exposes tools, systems, hands, machines, decisions, and consequences.

When these qualities conflict with novelty, prefer the qualities.

## Translate references without copying

Use references as problem-solving principles:

- RRL / Double RL: material truth and provenance.
- Texas Monthly: editorial judgment, hierarchy, photography, wit, and specificity.
- Filson: durable utility made handsome.
- early Southwest Airlines: clear information, democratic warmth, and confident wit.
- Apple at its best: precision, editing, restrained motion, and invisible finish.

Choose two or three systems, not all of them by default. Never copy proprietary fonts, marks, images, campaigns, pages, compositions, trade dress, or language. Never imitate a named photographer, designer, or writer. Translate high-level traits into original decisions grounded in the subject.

## Build the visual language

Let evidence create atmosphere.

- Draw material cues from indigo, leather, canvas, walnut, mahogany, blued steel, riveted aluminum, stone, oxblood, olive, brass, paper, and ink only when the subject supports them.
- Use editorial serif type for story and authority, plain sans for interface and summaries, condensed type sparingly for departments and dates, and mono only for meaningful metadata such as coordinates, serials, tail numbers, specifications, or file IDs.
- Build hierarchy through type scale, placement, whitespace, image scale, captions, and an editorial grid before adding decoration.
- Use generous margins, useful labels, meaningful numbering, and deliberate asymmetry.
- Avoid equal-size card grids when the content is not equal in importance.
- Prefer sharp, simple geometry. Avoid rounded-card and pill-heavy SaaS patterns unless function requires them.
- Keep motion short, purposeful, accessible, and subordinate to hierarchy.

## Direct photography as evidence

Favor observed, documentary images:

- real work, people, weather, tools, machines, rooms, roads, runways, workshops, landscapes, and consequences
- available light, environmental context, tactile details, and useful imperfection
- people doing something rather than posing as something
- captions that add names, place, date, process, credit, specification, or consequence

Preserve meaningful grain, tonal character, and source imperfections. Do not fabricate patina, damage, blur, flash falloff, or age. Do not use generic lifestyle imagery to manufacture mood.

Treat place as a rule set that can change proportion, light, rhythm, material, and language. Do not reduce Texas or any other place to scenery, costume, or cliché.

## Write like reporting

- Start with place, work, a problem, evidence, or consequence; do not start with greatness.
- Use plainspoken intelligence, specific nouns, active verbs, chronology, and earned feeling.
- Understate adjectives.
- Present the subject as a steward, builder, operator, maker, host, guide, musician, craftsman, or witness—not a self-appointed hero.
- Show people, tools, environments, routines, scars, decisions, costs, and outcomes.
- Preserve uncertainty. Narrow or remove claims that evidence cannot support.
- Write original prose. Extract principles from references; never mimic a named writer's sentences or signature voice.

## Adapt by project

### Texas Aviation Partners

Prioritize institutional legibility, operational credibility, warmth, and exactness. Lead with runways, hangars, parcels, maps, aircraft, infrastructure, and people at work. Structure case studies as **place -> constraint -> work -> evidence -> outcome**. Do not let heritage atmosphere interfere with navigation or trust.

### Wimberly Custom Guitars

Keep the instrument as the hero. Lead with wood, lacquer, metal, wiring, wear, hands, complete instruments, and construction details. Treat each guitar as a provenance record: woods, pickups, finish, hardware, date, and builder notes. Never invent crests, stamps, dates, or workshop history.

### Preston Wimberly portfolio and archive

Lead with selected work and build an edited table of contents rather than a service pitch. Make context, ownership, collaborators, artifacts, credits, dates, decisions, observable change, and outcomes easy to scan. Do not give unrelated projects equal visual weight.

### Music and session work

Document musicianship rather than celebrity. Lead with the room, instrument, hands, setup, soundcheck, road, and work between performances. Keep the result close, capable, lived-in, and unposed.

### The Wild Feathers archive

Let chronology and provenance drive the experience. Lead with contact sheets, setlists, posters, motel photographs, backstage frames, studio documents, dates, and places. Preserve source wear only when genuine. Keep private or unresolved material private until rights and fact-checking are confirmed.

### Memoir and personal story

Write what happened, what it cost, what survived, and what changed. Let place and consequence carry the weight. Avoid redemption mythology, self-punishment, or inflated lessons.

## Work in an existing codebase

Read [references/implementation-notes.md](references/implementation-notes.md) before editing a repository.

Then:

1. Audit the current structure, content, tokens, type, spacing, image treatment, navigation, responsiveness, accessibility, and performance.
2. Preserve distinctive work and useful logic.
3. Refine hierarchy, sequence, typography, spacing, crops, captions, and copy before adding components or effects.
4. Keep semantic HTML, keyboard behavior, visible focus, accessible contrast, reduced motion, responsive images, and performance as part of the aesthetic.
5. Make concrete changes when implementation is requested; do not stop at mood-board commentary.
6. Verify desktop, mobile, and narrow layouts and fix the weakest quality first.

## Report decisions

For design, revision, audit, or implementation work:

- State the subject and evidence.
- Name the two or three reference systems actually used.
- Name the three or four target characteristics.
- Explain major choices through use, place, craft, or story.
- Flag generic, decorative, falsely nostalgic, inaccessible, or overly polished elements.
- Prefer editing over invention.

## Core doctrine

Build work that feels trusted before it feels admired. Use Texas restraint, editorial intelligence, working materials, and evidence over promotion. Quiet confidence comes from discipline rather than decoration.

The seven outcomes are:

- timeless: durable after ten years of use
- authentic: grounded in actual people, place, work, and materials
- masculine: direct, restrained, and competent without machismo
- expensive: achieved through proportion, editing, photography, materials, pacing, and finish
- honest: claims supported by proof
- reliable: orderly, consistent, legible, and calm
- capable: showing tools, systems, hands, machines, and consequences

## Reference hierarchy

Use each reference for a distinct problem:

- RRL / Double RL for material truth, provenance, and physical evidence
- Texas Monthly for editorial hierarchy, photography, wit, and specificity
- Filson for durable utility and field credibility
- early Southwest Airlines for warm confidence and clear democratic information
- Apple at its best for precision, editing, restrained motion, and finish

Borrow principles, not costumes. Never combine every reference automatically, and never copy protected expression.

## Evidence and visual system

Every choice must be earned by use, place, craft, or story. Find real evidence before creating atmosphere. A worn surface matters only when someone wore it; a serial number matters only when it identifies something; a hangar matters only when that hangar belongs to the story.

Use physical materials as cues, not a mandatory palette: indigo, leather, canvas, wood, blued steel, riveted aluminum, stone, oxblood, olive, brass, paper, and ink.

Assign typography by role. Let editorial serif type carry story and authority; plain sans carry interface and summaries; condensed type carry occasional departments or folios; mono carry real technical metadata. Use editorial grids, generous margins, varied photographic scale, useful captions, measured spacing, and asymmetry only when earned.

Think magazine, field manual, catalog, or architectural monograph. Avoid digital decoration without a job.

## Photography, place, and voice

Photograph observed reality: people working, actual weather, available light, surfaces, rooms, landscape, machines, tools, and consequences. Preserve imperfection only when it belongs to the source.

Let place determine proportion, light, material, rhythm, and language. Hard desert light can create exposure and restraint; adobe and shadow can create quiet refinement; large landscapes can make people small; site and horizontality can unify a system. Apply these as compositional principles, never as imitations or regional costume.

Write like reporting. Use plainspoken intelligence, specific nouns, active verbs, place, chronology, consequence, and earned feeling. Understate adjectives. Replace broad claims with verifiable details. Do not imitate named writers or photographers.

## Project recipes

- **Institutional aviation:** established, operational, warm, exact; lead with infrastructure, maps, aircraft, and people at work.
- **Guitar workshop:** keep the instrument central; document materials, construction, specifications, date, and builder notes.
- **Creative portfolio:** story first; lead with context, artifacts, ownership, credits, dates, decisions, and outcomes.
- **Music/session work:** document musicianship and labor rather than celebrity.
- **Archive:** let chronology, provenance, dates, places, and source condition structure the work.
- **Memoir:** emphasize place, consequence, repair, and forward motion without mythology.

## Removal list

Remove fake distressing, invented patina, costume nostalgia, Western clichés, sepia sentiment, generic stock, mood without evidence, excessive cards or pills, gradients used only as atmosphere, motion standing in for hierarchy, and polish that erases the subject.
